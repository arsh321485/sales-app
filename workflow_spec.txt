2. The Most Important Business Rules
Create the product-specific Lead ID when a prospect is first entered into Outreach. Numbering is sequential per product (VF001, VF002; GM001, GM002; GD001, GD002; AI001, AI002; TMP001, TMP002).
The same Lead ID must remain attached from Outreach through Lead, Follow-up, Meeting, Demo, Requirement, Proposal, Pricing, Opportunity, Negotiation and Closure. Never create a new ID just because the record moves to another stage.
If the same company enters the pipeline for another product, create a separate product-specific Lead ID and link the records to the same company/contact.
Sales Stage and Activity are different: stage tells management where an opportunity stands; activities record what happened or what needs to happen.
Do not force a fixed sequence. A requirement, proposal, meeting, demo, pricing request, document request or follow-up may happen at any point and multiple times.
Avoid duplicate data entry. Selecting an existing Lead ID should automatically bring forward company, contact, product, owner, previous activities and client history. Users enter only details relevant to the new activity.

3. Main End-to-End Workflow
Login  ↓  Role-based dashboard  ↓  Outreach / import leads  ↓  Generate Lead ID  ↓  Qualify and assign lead  ↓  Record any activity  ↓  Handle requests and documents  ↓  Opportunity  ↓  Negotiation  ↓  Won / Lost / On Hold  ↓  Analytics
User logs in; the application displays the pages and actions allowed by their role.
A prospect is manually entered or imported from MapLeadGenerator (integration details in Section 7).
The user selects the product. The application creates the next product-specific Lead ID.
The lead is qualified, classified Hot/Warm/Cold, assigned to an owner and given a next action/follow-up where applicable.
Against the same Lead ID, the team can add follow-ups, meetings, demos, requirements, client requests, proposals, pricing, documents, NDA, SOW or other activities in any order.
When a client requests something, create a Client Request. Notify the owner and show the appropriate SharePoint resource where relevant.
The assigned member completes the action, records the result and marks it complete/sent. A follow-up can be created manually or automatically.
When the deal is serious, create/update an Opportunity with value, probability, expected closure date and next action.
Track negotiation and close as Won, Lost or On Hold.
For Won deals, record final value, closure date and revenue; update target, product/member/team analytics and the Master Dashboard.

7. Integrations: MapLeadGenerator and Sendy
7.1 MapLeadGenerator — Lead Generation and Excel View
Goal: allow users to obtain lead lists from MapLeadGenerator and view the resulting leads/Excel sheet inside this application, instead of keeping the lead-generation output disconnected from the sales workflow.
Open Lead Generation  ↓  Connect to / open MapLeadGenerator  ↓  Generate or select lead list  ↓  Import Excel or synced records  ↓  Preview and validate  ↓  Map columns  ↓  Choose product and owner  ↓  Import leads  ↓  Create/attach Lead IDs  ↓  Track in Sales
Provide a Lead Generation / MapLeadGenerator page with an option to connect to MapLeadGenerator and retrieve or upload its lead output.
Show the generated lead Excel sheet or a spreadsheet-style preview inside the application. Allow users to inspect rows and columns before import.
Support Excel file upload as a fallback if direct API/connector access is not available. Do not assume a specific API exists; the developer should confirm MapLeadGenerator's supported API/export and authentication method.
Validate required fields, identify blank or malformed values, and show import errors before confirming import.
Allow column mapping for company, contact person, designation, email, phone, LinkedIn, country/region (if supplied), source, campaign and other available columns. Preserve additional source columns where practical.
Before creating new leads, check for likely duplicates using available identifiers such as email, LinkedIn URL and company/contact. Show possible duplicates for review; do not silently create duplicate records.
During import, user selects the product and may assign an owner/campaign. Create the product-specific Lead ID for each accepted new prospect. If an existing record is matched, let the user link/update it according to permissions and preserve its existing Lead ID.
Save import source, import date, source file/list name, selected product, imported-by user, successful rows, skipped rows and error rows for audit/reporting.
Imported leads must appear in Outreach/Leads, use the same workflow as manually entered leads, and feed dashboards/analytics. Keep the original source data available where possible.
Developer decision required: confirm MapLeadGenerator API availability, authentication, rate limits, data fields and whether a live connection or export/import is supported. The application must support Excel upload if a live integration is not available.
7.2 Sendy — Email Outreach Campaign Analytics
Goal: integrate Sendy so the application can display email outreach campaigns and their analytics alongside the relevant product, campaign, lead and follow-up records.
Connect Sendy  ↓  Retrieve/select campaign  ↓  Match campaign to product and lead list  ↓  Sync campaign/contact results  ↓  Display campaign dashboard  ↓  Identify replies/engagement where data is available  ↓  Create follow-up tasks  ↓  Report conversions
Provide Sendy connection settings for the authorized account/API credentials and a safe way to store secrets. Confirm the supported Sendy API/version and required permissions before implementation.
Show campaign-level analytics, with filters for date range, product, campaign and owner where the underlying data supports those filters.
Display available campaign metrics, including sent/delivered, opens, unique opens, clicks/unique clicks, bounces, unsubscribes and other metrics exposed by Sendy. Clearly distinguish unavailable metrics; do not fabricate values.
Provide a campaign detail view with contact-level status/results where Sendy makes those data available, and a campaign-to-lead mapping using email and/or other reliable identifiers.
Link a Sendy campaign to one of the five products and, where possible, to the imported MapLeadGenerator list, outreach record and assigned owner.
Show email outreach analytics on the Master Dashboard and in Analytics. Support product-wise and campaign-wise comparison, trend charts, and drill-down from a KPI to the relevant campaign/contact records.
Use campaign results to help users manage follow-ups. Contacts needing follow-up should be identifiable, and a user should be able to create/record Follow-up 1, Follow-up 2 or another follow-up against the same Lead ID.
Do not automatically treat an open or click as a qualified lead or a reply. Engagement can inform prioritization, but qualification and closure should follow the sales process unless a confirmed response signal is available.
Handle sync failures, duplicate events and stale data clearly. Show last successful sync time and allow authorized users to refresh/sync where supported.
Respect unsubscribe/suppression status from Sendy. Do not re-add unsubscribed contacts to an outreach audience through an import or sync.
Developer decision required: verify which campaign, subscriber and event metrics Sendy's available API exposes for this account. If a required metric cannot be retrieved directly, label it unavailable or use an authorized export/import approach rather than estimating it.

Meetings
Fields: Lead ID; Client; Company; Product; Owner; Date; Time; Meeting type; Participants; Status; Notes; Outcome; Next action.
Meetings are activities, not mandatory stages. A meeting may occur before or after a proposal, requirement, demo, pricing or any other activity.
Demos
Fields: Lead ID; Product; Client; Demo date; Participants; Owner; Status; Feedback; Interest level; Requirements identified; Next action.
A demo may happen before or after a proposal or requirement.
Requirements
Requirements may be captured before, during or after a meeting; before/after a demo; after a proposal; during negotiation; or at any other point.
Fields: Lead ID; Product; Requirement type; Requirement description; Business requirement; Technical requirement; Scope; Timeline; Budget; Decision maker; Status; Notes; Next action; Date received.
Allow multiple requirements against the same Lead ID.
Client Requests
Requests are independent activities and may occur at any point.
Request types: proposal; pricing; presentation; company profile; brochure; case study; technical documentation; NDA; SOW; agreement; other document; other information.
Every request is linked to the same Lead ID.

Proposal requests can happen at any point and can repeat. Support initial, revised, commercial and technical proposals under the same Lead ID.

Sales documents remain in SharePoint; the application stores and displays links. The member should not have to search SharePoint manually.

19. Integration and Data Quality Rules
All sales activities, client requests, documents, follow-ups and opportunities must reference the same Lead ID wherever applicable.
MapLeadGenerator imports and Sendy campaign data should be linked to products/campaigns and leads only where a reliable match exists; show unmatched records for review rather than guessing.
Keep audit information for imports, syncs and document sends where described in this specification.
Avoid duplicate data entry and duplicate leads. Preserve existing Lead IDs when matching an existing record.
Respect role permissions for viewing, importing, editing, syncing, assigning and reporting.
Show integration errors and last successful sync time; never present unavailable integration metrics as if they were real.

21. Core Operating Principle
TRACK  ↓  ASSIGN  ↓  NOTIFY  ↓  ACT  ↓  UPDATE  ↓  ANALYZE  ↓  CLOSE  ↓  MEASURE REVENUE
The application should not merely store information; it should guide the team. It must answer: What happened? (Activity Timeline) What do I do next? (My Work) What does the client need? (Client Requests) What document should I send? (Sales Resources / SharePoint) Where is the opportunity? (Sales Stage / Opportunity) What is the team doing? (Team Analytics) How much revenue have we generated? (Revenue Analytics) Are we achieving the target? (Revenue Target) What needs attention? (Notifications)
